## 1. The base paper

**Jia, H., Cai, D., Yang, J., Qian, W., Wang, C., Li, X., Yang, S. (2023).**
*Efficient and privacy-preserving image classification using homomorphic
encryption and chunk-based convolutional neural network.*
**Journal of Cloud Computing 12(1), article 175.**
DOI [10.1186/s13677-023-00537-0](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13677-023-00537-0) ·
CC BY 4.0 · verified via Crossref.

### What it does

A four-stage pipeline: resize to 112×112 and histogram-equalise; split the
image into 28×28 chunks (a 4×4 grid of 16); compute a Sobel gradient per chunk
and keep those above a threshold; encrypt the retained chunks under CKKS and
classify them with a small CNN — one 7×7 convolution, then fully connected
layers 256→128 and 128→6. ReLU is used in the plaintext model and a **square**
activation in the encrypted one.

### What it reports

Table 3 is the headline comparison:

| Method | Dataset | Accuracy (%) | Activation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-label Tumor | TCGA | 85 | softmax |
| Mish Function | Fashion-MNIST | 84.68 | Approximated Mish |
| Mish Function | CIFAR-10 | 70.2 | Approximated Mish |
| **Our Method (Jia et al.)** | **NEU-CLS** | **97.1** | **Square** |

Table 1 reports CKKS key sizes (polynomial modulus 16384 for 28×28 inputs;
Galois keys 806 MB). Table 2 reports encryption time: 484 ms for a whole
112×112 image, 345 ms for all 16 chunks, 235 ms for the feature-bearing chunks
only.

### Documented weaknesses

These are stated because they shape what a fair comparison can claim, not to
disparage the work.

1. **Table 3 compares across different datasets.** The three reference rows are
   TCGA genomics, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10; only the authors' own row is
   NEU-CLS. An accuracy gap between a steel-defect benchmark and a cancer-genomics
   benchmark is not evidence of a better method.
2. **No precision, recall or F1 is reported for NEU-CLS.** The paper's NEU-CLS
   results appear only as figures (Fig. 9, Fig. 10), not as a table of values.
3. **Train/validation/test split sizes, epoch count, learning rate and batch
   size are not reported**, so the 97.1% figure cannot be situated against a
   known evaluation protocol.
4. **The chunk size is internally inconsistent**: the text says 28×28 in one
   place and 28×26 in two others. Only 28×28 is consistent with Table 2's "16
   chunks" at 112×112.
5. **The CKKS acronym is expanded incorrectly** as "Complex Key-Value Storage"
   in one section and correctly as Cheon–Kim–Kim–Song in another.
6. **The convolution channel count is never given**, and no stated pooling
   arithmetic produces the claimed 256-unit fully connected input from a 7×7
   convolution over 112×112.
7. **The paper states the network was "retrained with the encrypted training
   set."** Training (as opposed to inference) over CKKS ciphertexts is
   extraordinarily expensive and is not demonstrated; we read this as training
   in plaintext with the encryption-compatible operations.
8. **Encryption is reported to *improve* accuracy** over plaintext
   classification. CKKS is an approximate scheme; it adds numerical error. An
   accuracy gain from encryption is not explained and is the opposite of what
   the mechanism predicts.

## 2. Comparison matrix — steel surface defect classification

NEU-CLS is a small (1,800 image), visually well-separated benchmark, and the
published results are **saturated**: several independent methods report ≥99%.
This is essential context for interpreting any new result on it.

| Work | Year | Dataset | Architecture | Reported accuracy | Privacy | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Song & Yan [S13] | 2013 | NEU-CLS (origin) | Completed LBP + NN classifier | — (originating paper) | none | Defines the dataset |
| Jia et al. [J23] | 2023 | NEU-CLS | Chunk CNN + CKKS | 97.1 | CKKS on image chunks | **Base paper** |
| He et al. [H16] | 2016 | ImageNet | ResNet | — | none | Backbone lineage |
| Tan & Le [T19] | 2019 | ImageNet | EfficientNet (compound scaling) | — | none | Our global branch |
| Hu et al. [H18] | 2018 | ImageNet | Squeeze-and-Excitation | — | none | Channel-gating precedent |

**Saturation context (from the published literature on NEU-CLS, see §5 for the
verification caveat):** shallow attention CNNs, wavelet-texture descriptors and
modern backbones all report 99%+ on NEU-CLS, with some reporting 100% under
limited-data protocols. Our own measurement is consistent with this: a
pretrained EfficientNet-B0 reaches 100% test accuracy on our split within two
epochs. **Any architectural comparison conducted at full training size on
NEU-CLS is therefore at ceiling and cannot discriminate between components** —
which is precisely why this project's ablation is run in a reduced-data regime
(see `reports/technical_report.md`, §Ablation protocol).

## 3. Comparison matrix — privacy-preserving neural inference

| Work | Year | Scheme | What is encrypted | Activation | Relevance to SAGE-HE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cheon et al. [C17] | 2017 | **CKKS** (original) | — | — | The scheme we use |
| Jia et al. [J23] | 2023 | CKKS | image chunks | square | Base paper |
| Lee et al. [L22] | 2022 | RNS-CKKS | full network | minimax polynomial approx. of sign | Higher-degree alternative to square |
| Elkhatib et al. [E25] | 2025 | CKKS | restructured CNN | degree-4 polynomial Softplus | **Closest prior art on the boundary design** |

**[E25] PRISM** (Elkhatib, Sekmen & Hasan, arXiv:2511.07807, 11 Nov 2025;
verified via the arXiv API) reports 94.4% on CIFAR-10 at 2.42 s per encrypted
sample using a degree-4 polynomial activation under CKKS. It is the most
directly comparable system-level design: a CNN restructured so that only the
homomorphism-compatible part is evaluated under encryption.

## 4. Novelty assessment — stated narrowly

The brief asks whether the proposed combination has been published before. The
honest answer is **partly yes**, and the claim is scoped accordingly.

### What is NOT novel

* **Splitting a network so that only a small polynomial head runs under
  encryption.** This is established practice and is the core of PRISM [E25].
  SAGE-HE's client/server boundary is *not* claimed as novel.
* **The square activation for CKKS compatibility.** Used by the base paper
  [J23] and originating with CryptoNets-style designs; [L22] documents its
  accuracy limitations and the higher-degree alternatives.
* **Dual-branch global/local architectures, and gated fusion as a mechanism.**
  Channel-wise gating descends from Squeeze-and-Excitation [H18]; dual-branch
  global/local designs are widespread in defect detection and segmentation.
* **Using EfficientNet [T19] on NEU-CLS.** Standard practice.
* **Sobel gradient analysis for chunk selection.** This is exactly [J23].

### The proposed architectural novelty did not survive its own ablation

The one genuinely new construction here was the learned, differentiable,
Sobel-conditioned chunk attention — replacing [J23]'s non-differentiable
`|∇| > τ` rule with `aᵢ = MLP(fᵢ) + λ·êᵢ`, where λ is a learnable scalar trust
on the gradient prior. The intent was that λ would act as a readout: drive it
to zero and the model is telling us the prior is useless.

**Measured on NEU-CLS, it does not work, and we do not claim it.**

* Against its own EfficientNet-B0 backbone, under matched seeds and matched
  data at 61 training images, the full architecture **loses on every seed** —
  0.6 to 7.0 points of macro F1, mean −4.08. Adding the multi-scale local
  branch is likewise negative on every seed. These two contrasts are
  sign-consistent and are what the conclusion rests on.
* At 122 training images the gap closes to +0.55 points and changes sign across
  seeds: the architecture and the bare backbone become indistinguishable as
  both approach the dataset's ceiling. The components are therefore clearly
  harmful when labelled data is scarce and neutral once it is not; no measured
  regime shows a reliable benefit.
* The finer attribution — how much of that is the chunk attention versus the
  Sobel prior, taken separately — does **not** separate cleanly at three seeds
  and 61 training images. The point estimates go the same way, but the signs
  disagree across seeds, so neither is claimed individually. We can say the
  prior is not demonstrably helping; we cannot say it is demonstrably harming.
* λ never moves more than 0.01 from its initialisation in any run, so it
  cannot serve as the readout it was designed to be. One unregularised scalar
  among 4.7 M parameters receives negligible gradient.

Full numbers and the mechanical explanation are in
`reports/technical_report.md` §6.3–6.4. The architectural claim is withdrawn.

### What is defensible as a contribution

1. **A reproduction and critical audit of [J23] that the original does not
   provide**: macro precision/recall/F1, per-class metrics, a confusion matrix,
   confidence intervals, a paired significance test, and explicit documentation
   of the eight under-specifications listed in §1 — including the finding that
   the published 97.1% does not reproduce from the paper's own description.
2. **An evaluation protocol that remains informative on a saturated benchmark.**
   NEU-CLS is at ceiling for any modern pretrained backbone, so component
   comparisons at full training size rank noise. Measuring in a reduced-data
   regime chosen from the measured learning curve is what made the negative
   result above visible at all — a single-seed full-data comparison would have
   shown 100% everywhere and concluded, wrongly, that the components were fine.
3. **Genuine CKKS inference with a quantified exactness bound.** Not merely
   "encrypted and plaintext agreed on this test set", but: the error consumes a
   measured fraction of the decision margin, so agreement is a bound rather
   than a coincidence of the sample.
4. **An explicit, honest threat-model comparison.** [J23] encrypts image chunks;
   SAGE-HE encrypts a compressed feature vector. These protect different things
   and are not interchangeable. The technical report states what each does and
   does not protect, rather than implying equivalence.

### What is explicitly NOT claimed

* **Not claimed: an architectural advance.** The proposed components are not
  supported by the evidence on this dataset.
* Not claimed: that SAGE-HE's privacy model is equivalent to, or stronger than,
  encrypting the image itself. It is weaker in an important respect — the
  client must run and therefore hold the feature extractor.
* Not claimed: absolute novelty of any individual component.
* Not claimed: that beating a reproduced baseline is the same as beating the
  published 97.1%, which was obtained on an unknown split — nor that the margin
  over that baseline reflects the proposed components, since it is confounded
  with a pretrained backbone the baseline does not have.

## 5. Sources consulted but NOT cited as evidence

During the review, search results surfaced several works that are highly
relevant but which could not be resolved to verifiable publication metadata
within this project, or which are very recent preprints. They are recorded here
for completeness and transparency, and are deliberately **excluded** from the
comparison matrices and from any claim:

* A 2026 SSRN preprint reporting calibrated CKKS homomorphic inference on
  NEU-CLS (99.26% accuracy, macro F1 0.9920, ~138 s/image). If it holds up,
  it is closer prior art than anything in §3 — but it is an unrefereed preprint
  posted days before this work and we could not independently verify its
  metadata, so no claim here rests on it.
* Reported NEU-CLS results in the 99.6–99.9% range from shallow attention CNNs
  and wavelet texture descriptors. These informed the **saturation** argument in
  §2, which our own measurement independently confirms; the specific numbers are
  attributed to "the published literature" rather than to a verified citation.
