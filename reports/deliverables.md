## 2. What was actually achieved

* **Trained and evaluated both models over 3 seeds** on one identical
  held-out split: SAGE-HE 100.00%, reproduced baseline 93.98%.
* **Reproduced and audited the base paper**, documenting eight under-specifications
  and the finding that its published 97.1% does not reproduce from its own description.
  That finding was then **attacked rather than defended**: 4 further
  baselines vary the two things the paper leaves unspecified (convolution width
  and activation). 
  The best of them reaches 95.00%, still short of 97.1%, so the gap is
  not explained by those assumptions.
* **Verified the dataset rather than trusting it**: conformance to the published
  NEU-CLS specification, exact-duplicate removal, and a near-duplicate leakage audit
  that showed the apparent leakage was an illumination artefact.
* **Genuine CKKS encrypted inference**: 100.00% agreement with
  plaintext over 360 test images, with the error consuming only
  0.79% of the tightest decision
  margin — a bound, not a coincidence of the sample.
* **An evaluation protocol that works on a saturated benchmark**, derived from the
  measured learning curve rather than guessed.
* **A complete, reproducible pipeline**: one command regenerates every number,
  figure, diagram, report section and slide.

## 3. What did not work (reported, not buried)

* **The full SAGE-HE architecture never *materially* outperforms its own EfficientNet-B0 backbone at any training-set size measured. It is materially worse at 5% (-4.08 pts); at 10% (+0.55 pts) the gap falls inside the seed-to-seed spread and changes sign across seeds, because both arms are approaching the dataset's ceiling. The proposed components are therefore clearly harmful when labelled data is scarce, and indistinguishable from the bare backbone once it is not. No measured regime shows a reliable benefit.**

  * adding the local branch: -2.82 points macro F1 (consistent on every seed)
  * adding chunk attention: -1.26 points macro F1 (**not** sign-consistent — not claimed)
  * adding the Sobel prior: -1.28 points macro F1 (**not** sign-consistent — not claimed)
  * full model vs its own backbone: -4.08 points macro F1 (consistent on every seed)
* **λ never learned.** It moves at most 0.0097 from
  its initialisation, so it cannot serve as the readout it was designed to be.
* **Training collapses** in some very-low-data configurations; collapsed runs are
  flagged and excluded from robust statistics rather than silently averaged in.
* **The design choices are mostly indistinguishable, not wrong.** Several
  variants have a higher mean than the specified configuration
  (89.38% macro F1), but seed-to-seed spread at 61 training images is
  several points and swallows almost every gap. They are reported as
  indistinguishable rather than ranked — see the technical report, §6.5.

The architectural claim is withdrawn. Two contrasts hold on every seed and carry
that conclusion — adding the local branch costs accuracy, and the full model scores
below its own backbone. The finer attributions (chunk attention vs. Sobel prior
taken separately) do not separate cleanly at three seeds and 61 training images,
and are not claimed.

What the evidence supports is EfficientNet-B0 plus compression plus the CKKS head —
the privacy architecture without the extra feature machinery.

## 4. Limitations

* **Single dataset.** NEU-CLS only; no cross-dataset validation.
* **Saturated benchmark.** Component conclusions come from a reduced-data regime and should not be extrapolated to full-data behaviour.
* **Small test set.** 360 images; one image is 0.28 accuracy points.
* **No GPU.** All training and all latencies are CPU measurements on 4 vCPU.
* **The privacy model is weaker than the base paper's goal.** The client holds the feature extractor; only the server side of the boundary is protected.
* **The baseline is a reconstruction**, not the authors' code.
* **No circuit privacy.** Noise flooding is not implemented.

## 5. Remaining blockers

* **No GPU was available in this environment.** The brief specifies GPU training;
  everything was trained on CPU instead. The code selects CUDA automatically where
  present and needs no change on a GPU host.
* **`Privacy_concern.pdf` was referenced by the brief but never supplied.** The base
  paper is open access (CC BY 4.0), so it was retrieved from the publisher directly,
  including its tables — no analysis depends on the missing attachment.
* **Kaggle credentials were not available**, so NEU-CLS was obtained from a public
  mirror and verified against the published specification instead of trusted.
